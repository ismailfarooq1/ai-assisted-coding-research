\section{Literature Review}
\label{sec:literature}

\subsection{Scope and strength of evidence}
This is a focused literature review with a search cutoff of 8 November 2026,
not a systematic review or an exhaustive novelty assessment. Searches combined
terms for repository context files, coding agents, instruction adherence,
efficiency and maintenance. Recent directly relevant work was prioritized,
while earlier evaluation work was retained where it informs the experiment.
Conference, journal and workshop records were checked against primary
publication, conference or author pages. An arXiv version is identified
separately from a confirmed publication venue.

Ten directly relevant empirical papers are considered below. A dataset
publication, an evaluation benchmark and statistical-method references supply
supporting methods. For the
central papers, the review examines the relevant methods, results and stated
limitations. GHAgentFiles is considered at the conference-description and
dataset-metadata level; SWE-bench supplies an evaluation precedent from its
official description. External code, datasets and published numerical results
have not been independently audited or reproduced. A paper's reported result
is therefore attributed to its authors rather than presented as our own
verified experiment.

\subsection{The newest directly applicable proposal}
\label{sec:template}
\citet{shanto2026}, in a preprint posted on 2 November 2026, propose an instruction
template and test coverage and alignment of generated files. They do not evaluate
coding-task performance (Sections~6 and~9). They also distinguish divergent
guidance from contradictions. Their framework is a preparation candidate;
operational benefits remain unestablished.

\subsection{Experimental findings: efficiency and task success}
\label{sec:experiments}
\citet{lulla2026}, presented at the ICSE Journal Ahead Workshop, compare
with-file and without-file conditions over 124 pull-request tasks from ten
repositories. They report 28.64\% lower median runtime and 16.58\% fewer median
output tokens with \texttt{AGENTS.md}. However, Section~3.1.8 explicitly excludes
comprehensive semantic correctness evaluation; a manual sanity check does not
establish that patches satisfy all requirements. The finding motivates an
efficiency hypothesis, while leaving correctness and total workflow cost to be
tested. Lower output tokens alone do not establish lower total consumption.

\citet{gloaguen2026} offer complementary benchmark evidence. Their September
revision reports no statistically significant success improvement against the
no-file comparisons, while generated context increases inference cost by about
20--23\% on average. Developer-written context outperforms generated context,
but its comparison against no context is not significant. These are different
contrasts and should not be merged into a claim that human context universally
improves correctness. The publication record identifies a MemAgents workshop
paper; this review uses the later arXiv revision, especially Section~4.2 and
Tables~2--3.

\citet{khatri2026}, published at REALM 2026, compares absent, always-loaded and
selective context across two agents and three repositories. The study reports no
detectable correctness gain, with narrow process-efficiency effects. Its
limitations acknowledge limited power, a missing natural-discovery condition,
and selective corpora that differ in content as well as delivery. The paper's
descriptive equivalence bounds should not be read as a general powered proof
of equivalence. It is a close methodological precedent, but does not establish
how context performs in Memos or what maintaining it costs.

\citet{shepard2026}, in a June preprint, compare absent, static and
probe-refined guidance on SWE-bench Verified. Across four trials they report
33.0\% mean resolution with refined guidance, against 28.3\% with static
guidance and 25.5\% without guidance. Refinement uses synthetic probes rather
than scored benchmark tasks. The demonstrated benefit depends on the model and
execution budget; transfer to another model is harmful in their setup.
Guidance length also changes. This is positive evidence for testing a
\emph{preparation procedure}, not proof that any additional context helps.
The reviewed version is arXiv v2, revised 19 June 2026; no peer-reviewed venue
was established by the checked record.

\citet{kerbel2026}, published at AgenticDev alongside ASE, study context
authorship and delivery. Capable-author context improves a smaller agent's
file localization on deliberately constraint-matched tasks; that is not an
established bug-resolution improvement. Their threats section discloses topic
conditioning, possible author contamination, analysis deviations and unrun
conditions. Review, automatic extraction and long-term accumulation are not
validated by the experiment. This close overlap limits novelty claims for a
versioned project knowledge layer and motivates separating navigation,
correctness and measured upkeep. The accepted paper's source was reviewed;
its released experiment implementation and records were not audited.

\subsection{Structure, adherence and maintained artifacts}
\citet{mcmillan2026}, an arXiv preprint, manipulates four file-structure variables
across 1,650 coding-agent sessions. The study finds no detectable structural contrasts
for a simple annotation-adherence outcome. Some null contrasts have stronger
Bayesian support than others. Its dependent variable is a marker-insertion
proxy, not functional task correctness; the limitations discuss model/tool
confounding and transfer to demanding instructions. This is counterevidence
to assuming that reorganizing Markdown necessarily helps, and shows that
structural ablations already exist.

\citet{mohsenimofidi2026}, published at MSR, describe adoption, content,
presentation and evolution of project context files. Their observational study
finds varied organization and writing styles, alongside recurring topics.
Their conclusion calls for tests connecting content and presentation to agent
behavior and for investigation of code/context coevolution. The study motivates
an intervention, but does not identify a universally beneficial schema. The
absence of a common structure is not the absence of experimental comparisons.

\citet{chatlatanagulchai2026}, listed by their authors as a TOSEM publication,
characterize context files as maintained configuration-like artifacts. The
reviewed manuscript analyzes content and edit histories, but does not directly
trace which code changes cause context updates. Section~5.1 proposes studying
paired changes and synchronization tools. This supports observing actual
code/context maintenance episodes rather than inferring maintenance quality
or labor from edit counts alone.

\subsection{Organizational evidence and supporting resources}
\citet{denisov2026}, published at ASE, examine committed AI configuration and
software-quality outcomes. Their maturity analysis is associated with lower
quality costs in relevant repository strata, but the authors explicitly treat
the association as hypothesis-generating: engineering discipline and model
capability may explain part of it. This supplies an organizational motivation
for controlled comparisons, not a causal estimate of savings from adding
context files to Memos.

\citet{cardoen2026} provide GHAgentFiles, an ICSME dataset publication with
context-file histories and an accompanying extraction tool. It could support
later selection of realistic maintenance changes. Its availability does not
itself validate a maintenance intervention, and the dataset has not been
examined or downloaded for this study.

\citet{jimenez2024} provide an evaluation precedent in SWE-bench: issue-based
repository changes are assessed using executable tests. The Memos study can
adopt independent behavioral evaluation without claiming to reproduce that
benchmark or treating test-suite success as exhaustive software quality.

\subsection{What the combined evidence establishes}
The literature already contains comparisons of repository context and controlled
structural variations. A thesis claim that nobody has compared these approaches
would therefore be inaccurate. The findings are also not a simple vote for or
against context: studies change different information, use different agents and
measure different outcomes. Operational efficiency, annotation adherence,
document coverage, task correctness and project-level quality are distinct
constructs.

The defensible starting point is uncertainty about \emph{incremental practical
value under a specified workflow}. More guidance may reduce exploration, add
unnecessary work, repeat facts available in source, or become outdated. Those
are competing mechanisms to investigate. Publication recency improves relevance
to current practice but does not replace controls, independent correctness
checks or careful interpretation.
