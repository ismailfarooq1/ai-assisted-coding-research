\begin{abstract}
This research design asks whether project-context workflows improve coding-agent
outcomes beyond Memos's existing documented practice, and what preparation and
maintenance they require. The literature contains positive, negative and
inconclusive results; it does not establish a universal winning workflow.
The primary comparison defined here is independently verified task completion
under common execution limits. Paired task-level analysis, correction for
multiple comparisons and explicit decision rules distinguish superiority from
inconclusive evidence. Tokens, elapsed time and observed human effort are
reported alongside correctness, including preparation, failures and upkeep.
Methods A and B denote existing practice and deliberately prepared context;
method C remains undesigned. This document completes the literature synthesis
and specifies the comparison framework. It reports no Memos experiment results.
Concrete tasks, models, budgets and sample sizes require a subsequent frozen
execution protocol; no experimental configuration is changed here.
\end{abstract}
